AD BLOCKER PROJECT IDEA — EVIDENCE-DRIVEN PRIVACY PROTECTION
======================================================================

Document date: 17 November 2026 (Asia/Kolkata)
Document type: product and research charter
Working concept name: Evidence-Driven Privacy Shield
Brand status: not selected; the working name is not a trademark decision


0. DOCUMENT STATUS AND TRUTH RULES
==================================

This document describes the strongest responsible long-term idea for this
project. It is a direction, not a claim that every feature already exists and
not a promise that every research idea will ship.

Current source code, reproducible builds, exact-version browser evidence, and
reviewed release artifacts always outrank planning text. A feature moves from
idea to supported product only after its code, tests, browser behavior,
security and privacy impact, licensing, rollback, and known limitations are
recorded.

The reorganized baseline described here was pushed to main as commit e5260c9.
The active first-party project is under src/extension/. ZIP-derived Ghostery
and uBlock Origin trees under AnalyseFuture/ are preserved research and
forensic material. They are not trusted dependencies, active engines, or
approved sources for distribution. Their exact upstream revisions are not
known. Nothing in this idea authorizes copying, running, or shipping them.

Historical CI results show that earlier versions of the small Chrome preview
passed controlled tests. Those results remain useful history, but the
reorganized commit must be revalidated in a later testing task. No application
tests or builds were run while restructuring the repository or writing this
document, as requested by the owner.


1. THE IDEA IN ONE SENTENCE
===========================

Build a local-first, privacy-first ad and tracker blocker that begins with a
dependable Chrome Manifest V3 extension, earns every public claim through
repeatable evidence, minimizes damage to wanted content, gives users simple
reversible control, and uses AI only as carefully measured assistance for
testing, compatibility, and rule maintenance.


2. THE USER PROBLEM
===================

Advertising and tracking can make pages slower, more distracting, less
private, and sometimes less safe. Existing blockers can be powerful, but the
environment is difficult:

  - browsers restrict what extensions may observe and change;
  - websites and advertising delivery change continuously;
  - aggressive rules can hide legitimate content or break important actions;
  - filters, software, data, and assets have different licenses;
  - users need both simple controls and advanced, understandable recovery;
  - regional sites and languages receive uneven maintenance;
  - video advertising includes technically different categories that cannot
    be solved by one universal rule;
  - unsupported marketing claims make it hard to know what truly works.

The project should solve the practical problem, not merely create a large
feature list. Success means useful protection with low breakage, clear limits,
safe updates, and sustainable maintenance.


3. WHO THE PRODUCT SERVES
=========================

Everyday users
  Want fewer intrusive ads, pages that still work, and one clear protection
  control without learning filter syntax.

Privacy-conscious users
  Want tracker protection, minimal permissions, local processing, no hidden
  telemetry, and an understandable privacy policy.

Power users
  Want trusted-site exceptions, custom rules, optional lists, diagnostics,
  import/export, and reversible element selection.

Accessibility and low-resource users
  Need keyboard and screen-reader support, predictable layouts, low CPU and
  memory overhead, and protection that does not remove essential controls.

Regional and multilingual users
  Need locally relevant coverage reviewed by people who understand the sites,
  languages, and legitimate content being tested.

Maintainers, testers, and security researchers
  Need reproducible builds, small coherent modules, exact evidence, clear
  provenance, safe disclosure, and a fast way to reproduce regressions.


4. THE PRODUCT PROMISE
======================

The project will pursue the broadest useful protection it can demonstrate. It
will preserve wanted content, investigate missed ads, repair reproducible site
breakage, make controls reversible, and show limitations honestly.

It will compete through:

  1. measured blocking and tracker protection;
  2. low severity-weighted website breakage;
  3. privacy by default and no browsing-history business model;
  4. fast recovery when a rule, update, or setting fails;
  5. understandable basic controls plus an optional expert mode;
  6. accessible and efficient operation;
  7. transparent rule, dependency, and release provenance;
  8. regional coverage that is earned region by region;
  9. a compatibility program that continues after launch;
 10. evidence instead of “number one” or “100%” marketing.


5. NON-NEGOTIABLE PRINCIPLES
============================

Privacy by default
  Filtering and ordinary settings stay on the device. The default product
  does not upload browsing history, full URLs, page text, screenshots, account
  identifiers, cookies, or private page content.

Deterministic enforcement
  Browser-native rules and reviewed code decide what is blocked. A remote AI
  response is never required for normal page loading.

Wanted-content protection
  A missed ad is a defect to investigate; deleting a checkout button, login,
  article, player control, accessibility node, or other wanted content can be
  a more serious defect. Both must be measured.

Reversible control
  Global pause, per-site trust, rule undo, settings reset, last-known-good
  rollback, and clear error messages are product features, not afterthoughts.

Proof over claims
  Documentation separates present source, controlled browser evidence,
  limited real-site evidence, experimental results, and future ideas.

One coherent filtering pipeline
  The project will not combine several overlapping engines merely because
  their source is available. A single active architecture must have a named
  owner and a recorded decision.

Legal and supply-chain clarity
  Public availability is not permission to relicense or redistribute code,
  filter data, model weights, assets, or brands. Exact revisions, licenses,
  notices, dependencies, and data rights must be reviewed before integration.

Secure updates
  Filter data and application updates are versioned, validated, integrity
  checked, staged, observable, and reversible. Remote executable JavaScript is
  prohibited.

Sustainable scope
  Worldwide ambition requires continuous maintenance. A feature without an
  owner, evidence plan, and maintenance capacity is not ready to promise.


6. CURRENT STARTING POINT
=========================

The active repository contains a small original Chrome MV3 preview:

  - a Manifest V3 package under src/extension/;
  - a small packaged declarativeNetRequest starter ruleset;
  - a background service worker for settings and browser rule coordination;
  - local enabled state and trusted-site exceptions;
  - narrow, reversible cosmetic CSS;
  - popup and options interfaces;
  - settings validation, recovery, and reset behavior;
  - Node unit fixtures and a Playwright Chromium fixture suite;
  - dependency-light verification and build scripts;
  - CI, security, contribution, architecture, product, and research records.

It is not yet a mature commercial or worldwide blocker. It does not yet have:

  - an owner-approved project license and distribution model;
  - a verified production filter-list compiler and legal data catalog;
  - broad network/cosmetic syntax coverage;
  - safe production list updates and last-known-good rollback;
  - a mature custom-rule system or element picker;
  - representative worldwide compatibility evidence;
  - complete accessibility, permission, privacy, and security review;
  - a current release candidate or store approval;
  - verified competitor superiority;
  - guaranteed video-ad blocking;
  - approved desktop, DNS, or mobile products.


7. THE DIFFERENTIATED USER EXPERIENCE
=====================================

Simple mode
  The popup shows whether protection is active, whether the current site is
  trusted, what action will happen, and how to recover. Controls use plain
  language and remain keyboard accessible.

Recovery first
  When a site breaks, the user can pause protection for that site, reload, see
  which protection category may be involved, undo recent changes, and return
  to the last-known-good ruleset. Recovery should not require deleting the
  extension or losing all settings.

Advanced mode
  Expert users may inspect enabled lists, unsupported syntax, ruleset version,
  rule-budget use, custom rules, and diagnostics. Dangerous or experimental
  capabilities are separate, explained, and reversible.

Honest diagnostics
  Show only information supported by production browser APIs. If an exact
  blocked-request count is unavailable, explain the limitation or omit the
  number. Never display a fabricated counter.

Privacy-respecting reports
  A future breakage report is previewable and opt-in. It minimizes and redacts
  data before sending anything. Raw browsing history is never silently
  attached.

Global but locally reviewed coverage
  Language and region packs are visible choices with sources, maintainers,
  versions, and known limits. “Worldwide” is an ambition assembled from
  measured regional coverage, not one unsupported checkbox.


8. FUNCTIONAL ARCHITECTURE
==========================

The product should develop as six connected layers.

Layer A — Browser enforcement
  Chrome declarativeNetRequest performs browser-native network blocking.
  Content scripts perform narrowly scoped cosmetic filtering. Platform limits
  and permissions remain explicit.

Layer B — Local policy and controls
  A validated state model owns global protection, per-site exceptions,
  profiles, custom rules, migrations, recovery, and import/export. All state
  changes are serialized where concurrency could cause lost updates.

Layer C — Rule production
  Approved filter sources enter a build-time parser/compiler. It records
  provenance, supported and unsupported syntax, priority conflicts, duplicate
  rules, browser quotas, and deterministic output hashes.

Layer D — Safe update delivery
  Candidate rule bundles are versioned, integrity checked, tested, staged, and
  promoted only after gates pass. The extension retains a last-known-good
  version and can recover offline.

Layer E — Compatibility laboratory
  Synthetic fixtures and consent-respecting representative sites measure
  blocking, wanted-content preservation, performance, accessibility, browser
  behavior, regional gaps, updates, restart behavior, and recovery.

Layer F — Optional AI research
  Models may propose issue labels, candidate rules, tests, or explanations.
  Deterministic validators, privacy review, human review, isolated experiments,
  staged rollout, and rollback stand between any model output and users.

Conceptual flow:

  approved filter sources
    -> provenance and license registry
    -> parser/compiler
    -> syntax, conflict, quota, and integrity validation
    -> versioned candidate ruleset
    -> compatibility and security gates
    -> staged release
    -> browser-native enforcement
    -> local diagnostics and reversible user controls
    -> last-known-good rollback when needed

  optional AI experiment
    -> candidate suggestion only
    -> deterministic validation
    -> human review
    -> isolated test or canary
    -> accept, revise, or reject

AnalyseFuture/ has no active edge in this flow. Promotion from quarantine is a
separate, approval-gated provenance, license, security, and architecture task.


9. RULE, DATA, AND LICENSE GOVERNANCE
=====================================

Every integrated source should have a machine-readable record containing:

  - canonical source URL;
  - immutable commit, release, or content hash;
  - source, asset, dependency, and filter-data licenses;
  - required copyright and attribution notices;
  - intended use and files included in the distributed product;
  - supported syntax and deliberately omitted behavior;
  - update frequency and responsible maintainer;
  - integrity verification and rollback behavior;
  - security history and dependency risk;
  - decision record approving, deferring, or rejecting integration.

The first architecture decision must choose one active rule pipeline. Ghostery
and uBlock-derived snapshots may inform research only after their provenance
is verified. GPL, MPL, permissive code, and independently licensed list data
have different obligations. AI summaries are not legal verification.


10. COMPATIBILITY AS AN OPERATING SYSTEM
========================================

Ad blocking is not a one-time integration. The project needs a recurring
operating process:

  1. detect a missed ad, tracker leak, breakage, performance regression, or
     browser-policy change;
  2. reproduce it with a minimized, privacy-safe fixture where possible;
  3. classify the affected rule, site category, browser, version, region, and
     user action;
  4. propose the smallest deterministic fix;
  5. test both the desired block and legitimate negative controls;
  6. evaluate performance, accessibility, privacy, and rule-budget impact;
  7. review provenance and licensing when data or code changes;
  8. stage the change and retain rollback;
  9. record evidence and known limits;
 10. monitor support cost and reopen when new evidence appears.

Representative coverage should include ordinary publishing, commerce, search,
social, streaming, games, web applications, authentication, payments,
accessibility workflows, regional sites, and benign pages with ad-like names.
Testing must never depend on collecting unsuspecting users' browsing histories.


11. WHAT IS REALISTIC TO BUILD NOW
==================================

With current browser and engineering capabilities, the project can reasonably
build and verify:

  - a dependable Chrome MV3 package using browser-native DNR;
  - licensed network and cosmetic filter compilation within browser quotas;
  - clear unsupported-syntax and quota reports;
  - deterministic priority, exceptions, deduplication, and conflict rules;
  - global pause and per-site trust with reliable persistence and recovery;
  - safe settings migration, reset, backup, and validated import/export;
  - versioned filter updates, integrity checks, offline fallback, and rollback;
  - custom blocking and allow rules with preview, validation, and undo;
  - a reversible, accessibility-aware element picker;
  - tracker, nuisance, and regional profiles with measured breakage;
  - transparent diagnostics without fake statistics;
  - accessible popup/options experiences;
  - reproducible Chrome builds, staged releases, and vulnerability response;
  - controlled comparisons against current competitor versions;
  - optional Edge and Firefox adapters after Chrome operations are stable.

These are feasible capabilities, not automatically completed work. Each still
requires an accountable owner, implementation, review, evidence, and ongoing
maintenance.


12. WHAT IS NOT POSSIBLE OR MUST NOT BE PROMISED
=================================================

Permanent 100% blocking everywhere
  No blocker can responsibly guarantee every ad and tracker on every website,
  language, browser, device, account state, and future delivery method.

Universal YouTube or video-ad removal
  Some ads are ordinary network requests, some are overlays, some are client
  inserted, some are sponsorship segments, and some are inserted into the same
  authenticated or encrypted media stream as wanted content. When ad and
  content bytes are technically indistinguishable, an extension cannot promise
  perfect removal without risking wanted playback.

Zero false positives and zero website breakage
  Aggressive filtering and site compatibility are competing goals. The project
  can reduce, measure, and recover from breakage; it cannot guarantee none.

Zero-maintenance worldwide coverage
  Websites, lists, browsers, policies, and adversaries change. A one-time code
  merge cannot produce permanent global coverage.

Unrestricted interception under Chrome MV3
  Chrome controls available APIs, permissions, quotas, and store policy. An
  extension cannot behave like an unrestricted operating-system proxy.

Device-wide protection from one browser extension
  DNS, VPN, native desktop, mobile-app, and network-wide filtering require
  separate products, permissions, threat models, deployments, and tests.

Exact universal block counters
  Production MV3 APIs may not expose every event needed for a complete exact
  counter. Development-only information cannot justify a public fake number.

Automatic parity on every browser
  Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari, iOS, Android, and embedded browsers have
  different APIs, signing, policy, and extension models. Each target needs an
  independently tested adapter or separate product.

Guaranteed invisibility to anti-blocking systems
  Detection and evasion are adversarial and change over time. The product will
  not promise permanent invisibility or use unsafe tricks to chase it.

Autonomous AI publishing
  AI cannot safely or legally promote filters, selectors, scriptlets,
  dependencies, model weights, or releases without deterministic checks,
  provenance, license review, human approval, staged delivery, and rollback.

AI as an authenticity, security, or license oracle
  Model output may assist research, but it cannot prove a file is authentic,
  code is safe, a license is compatible, or a vulnerability is absent.

Privacy-perfect cloud page analysis
  Sending raw URLs, page text, screenshots, account state, or browsing history
  to a remote model contradicts the default privacy promise and cannot be made
  harmless merely by calling it AI.

Inappropriate or prohibited goals
  The project will not bypass paywalls, DRM, account controls, browser security
  boundaries, consent mechanisms, or store protections. It will not disguise
  malware, collect user histories for sale, or claim ownership of upstream
  copyrights.

Unsupported market claims
  “Best,” “number one,” “production ready,” and competitor-superiority claims
  are not allowed without dated, reproducible, comparable evidence.


13. DELIVERY ROADMAP — BUILD THE DETERMINISTIC CORE FIRST
=========================================================

Phase 0 — Ownership and trusted foundation

  - owner selects distribution model, project license, contributor terms, and
    brand process;
  - preserve the reorganized active baseline and quarantine boundary;
  - review imported provenance without executing untrusted lifecycle scripts;
  - compare realistic foundations and choose exactly one active pipeline;
  - record architecture, licensing, privacy, and permission decisions;
  - revalidate the active preview in the target browser in a later test task.

Exit gate:
  One approved architecture, reproducible active build, known legal boundary,
  current browser evidence, and no critical unexplained source provenance in
  the active product.

Phase 1 — Reliable Chrome alpha

  - finish settings consistency, concurrency, API failure, and recovery work;
  - complete real Chrome install, popup, options, reload, restart, accessibility,
    permission, privacy, and negative-control tests;
  - establish a legal initial filter-source registry and deterministic compiler;
  - measure DNR quota use and unsupported filter syntax;
  - produce a developer alpha with checksums and exact limitations.

Exit gate:
  The extension installs reproducibly, core controls work in the target Chrome
  version, known critical fixture breakage is resolved, and claims match data.

Phase 2 — Mature filtering and safe updates

  - add maintained network, tracker, cosmetic, nuisance, and regional profiles;
  - implement versioned updates, integrity validation, staged promotion,
    last-known-good rollback, and offline recovery;
  - add validated custom rules and reversible element selection;
  - establish a representative compatibility corpus and breakage severity model;
  - publish unsupported syntax and known limitations.

Exit gate:
  Dated coverage, false-positive, breakage, performance, and rollback evidence;
  every shipped data source has provenance and ownership.

Phase 3 — Product quality and controlled release

  - complete accessible onboarding and advanced settings;
  - add honest diagnostics and privacy-safe support reports;
  - perform security, supply-chain, permissions, privacy, and legal reviews;
  - establish release signing, artifact checksums, incident response, support,
    staged rollout, and rollback drills;
  - prepare accurate store assets and policies only after owner approval.

Exit gate:
  A reproducible release candidate passes the complete gate, high-priority
  findings are resolved, and an accountable operator can support the release.

Phase 4 — Measured competition

  - compare identical workloads against pinned current competitor releases;
  - measure blocking, wanted-content preservation, page breakage, performance,
    privacy, accessibility, recovery, and maintenance cost;
  - fix regressions and publish methodology, uncertainty, and omissions;
  - prioritize differentiators users actually value.

Exit gate:
  Reproducible results support narrowly worded public claims. No ranking is
  inferred from feature count or marketing copy.

Phase 5 — Approved expansion

  - adapt to Edge and Firefox with independent tests;
  - investigate video categories honestly;
  - decide whether mobile, desktop, or DNS deserve separate products;
  - expand regional stewardship only when qualified maintainers exist.

Exit gate:
  Each platform or research area has its own go/no-go decision, threat model,
  owner, cost model, and evidence. Expansion is never automatic.


14. AI IN THIS PROJECT: ALLOWED AND PROHIBITED ROLES
===================================================

AI is useful when it reduces review time or discovers reproducible defects. It
is dangerous when probabilistic output silently becomes enforcement.

Allowed, after evaluation:

  - cluster duplicate, redacted issue reports;
  - suggest a likely failure category;
  - generate candidate synthetic fixtures or fuzz cases;
  - suggest candidate filter rules or safe selectors for human review;
  - explain diagnostics in plain language;
  - find documentation inconsistencies;
  - assist localization under native-language review;
  - rank compatibility risk for a proposed deterministic change;
  - explore small on-device classifiers in isolated or opt-in experiments.

Prohibited by default:

  - uploading raw browsing activity or private page content;
  - blocking a page solely because a remote model says so;
  - generating and publishing rules without deterministic negative controls;
  - changing permissions, dependencies, licenses, or update endpoints silently;
  - executing model-generated code or remote JavaScript in the extension;
  - treating confidence scores as proof of safety;
  - hiding model errors or presenting estimates as exact facts;
  - allowing an AI agent to sign or release without accountable human approval.


15. FIRST-YEAR AI-ERA RESEARCH HORIZON
======================================

“First year” means the first twelve months after a staffed research program
begins. It is not a guaranteed calendar release. The deterministic filter and
rollback foundation remains higher priority.

Experiment A — Redacted issue and breakage triage

Accountable owner:
  Compatibility/QA lead, with privacy reviewer and ML research support.

Hypothesis:
  A model can group duplicate reports, identify likely rule categories, and
  reduce maintainer triage time without retaining sensitive browsing data.

Evaluation:
  A labeled historical/synthetic set; cluster purity; urgent-defect recall;
  misrouting rate; maintainer minutes saved; private-data leakage review.

Success gate:
  Material, repeatable time savings with acceptable urgent-case recall and no
  need for raw browsing histories.

Stop conditions:
  Private data is required; urgent issues are hidden or misgrouped; maintainers
  spend as much time correcting the model as they save; clusters drift badly.

Experiment B — Candidate filter and cosmetic-rule suggestions

Accountable owner:
  Filtering/rules lead; security and compatibility reviewers approve results.

Hypothesis:
  AI suggestions can shorten diagnosis while deterministic validation and
  human review protect users.

Evaluation:
  Held-out missed-ad and broken-site cases; supported syntax; block efficacy;
  wanted-content false positives; reviewer acceptance; reproducibility; time.

Success gate:
  Accepted suggestions resolve more verified cases per reviewer hour than the
  manual baseline without increasing severe regressions.

Stop conditions:
  Legitimate content is repeatedly hidden; suggestions require unsafe scripts;
  results are not reproducible; license/provenance is unclear; acceptance is
  too low to justify review cost.

Experiment C — Regression fixture and fuzz-case generation

Accountable owner:
  QA/test lead.

Hypothesis:
  Generated variations can find state, parser, rule-priority, DOM, migration,
  and recovery defects that hand-written fixtures miss.

Evaluation:
  Unique reproducible defects found; mutation coverage; negative-control
  quality; flake rate; duplicate rate; compute and review cost.

Success gate:
  The experiment finds valuable minimized cases and improves coverage without
  overwhelming reviewers or weakening assertions.

Stop conditions:
  Tests are mainly duplicate, trivial, flaky, non-deterministic, or harder to
  audit than equivalent hand-written fixtures.

Experiment D — On-device ambiguous-container classifier feasibility

Accountable owner:
  ML/runtime research lead, with performance, privacy, accessibility, and
  compatibility reviewers.

Hypothesis:
  A small local model might identify ambiguous visual ad containers that safe
  deterministic selectors miss.

Evaluation:
  Diverse held-out pages and languages; precision/recall; legitimate-content
  loss; task completion; CPU, memory, latency, battery, package size; offline
  behavior; adversarial examples; accessibility-tree impact.

Success gate:
  A shadow-mode or opt-in prototype provides measurable incremental benefit
  while meeting strict false-positive and device budgets.

Stop conditions:
  It needs page uploads, broad new permissions, or remote inference; wanted
  content is harmed; overhead is visible; a simpler deterministic rule performs
  similarly; model maintenance has no sustainable owner.

Experiment E — Documentation, accessibility, and localization assistance

Accountable owner:
  UX/accessibility lead and native-language reviewers.

Hypothesis:
  AI can help find unclear controls, missing labels, inconsistent claims, and
  translation gaps without deciding final policy language.

Evaluation:
  User-task completion; keyboard and screen-reader review; translation defect
  rate; misleading-claim rate; reviewer time.

Success gate:
  Human-reviewed assistance improves clarity or coverage with fewer defects
  and less effort than the baseline.

Stop conditions:
  Accessibility regressions appear; native reviewers are unavailable; legal or
  privacy wording is published without qualified human review.


16. SECOND-YEAR AI-ERA RESEARCH HORIZON
=======================================

These experiments begin only after the deterministic pipeline, compatibility
corpus, update rollback, privacy rules, and research governance are stable.

Experiment F — Breakage-risk ranking for rule changes

Owner:
  Compatibility lead plus ML research engineer.

Method and evaluation:
  Train or score only on approved versioned evidence; compare against simple
  deterministic heuristics; measure calibration, severe-breakage recall, false
  alarms, canary outcomes, drift, and maintainer time saved.

Success:
  High-risk changes are surfaced earlier and severe regressions decline without
  blocking ordinary safe updates.

Stop:
  Poor calibration, unexplained drift, too many false alarms, or no gain over
  transparent rules.

Experiment G — Regional and multilingual gap discovery

Owner:
  Rule/data steward plus named regional/native-language maintainers.

Method and evaluation:
  Use licensed or consented regional corpora; measure reproducible coverage
  gains, false positives, reviewer agreement, accessibility, and maintenance
  cost by region.

Success:
  A region gains verified coverage with sustainable local review and clear data
  rights.

Stop:
  No qualified reviewers, ambiguous data rights, systematic wanted-content
  harm, or maintenance cost exceeds available ownership.

Experiment H — Minimal reversible cosmetic-rule generation

Owner:
  Browser UI/filtering engineer with accessibility and QA review.

Method and evaluation:
  Generate the smallest candidate selector; test DOM variations, legitimate
  siblings, navigation, keyboard operation, and undo. Compare with the manual
  element-picker baseline.

Success:
  More correct reversible rules with no increase in severe hiding defects.

Stop:
  Broad/brittle selectors, inaccessible results, non-reproducible behavior, or
  insufficient advantage over deterministic picker logic.

Experiment I — Dynamic-page compatibility and repair suggestions

Owner:
  Browser compatibility lead.

Method and evaluation:
  Diagnose state changes on approved fixtures; suggest a minimal allow rule,
  exception, or cosmetic change; measure recovery rate, false repair, and page
  task completion. Suggestions remain human approved.

Success:
  Faster recovery from reproducible dynamic-page breakage without weakening
  unrelated protection.

Stop:
  Suggestions broadly disable protection, require unsafe injection, or cannot
  preserve authentication, payments, media, and accessibility tasks.

Experiment J — Category-specific video diagnostics

Owner:
  Video compatibility engineer with policy/legal and privacy reviewers.

Method and evaluation:
  Treat network ads, overlays, client-inserted segments, sponsorship segments,
  server-side insertion, and encrypted streams separately. Record exact
  browser, account type, region, media version, playback integrity, false
  skips, and privacy impact.

Success:
  A narrowly defined category becomes more measurable or controllable without
  harming wanted playback or accounts.

Stop:
  Ad and content are technically indistinguishable; wanted media is skipped;
  the method risks accounts, privacy, DRM, paywalls, or platform policy.

Experiment K — Assisted cross-browser adaptation

Owner:
  Browser-platform lead.

Method and evaluation:
  AI may propose adapter changes, but each browser must pass its native
  permissions, packaging, signing, security, and compatibility matrix.

Success:
  Reduced adaptation effort while platform-specific behavior remains explicit
  and independently verified.

Stop:
  Generated abstractions hide important API differences, weaken security, or
  cost more to maintain than focused adapters.


17. THREE-YEAR AND LONGER RESEARCH HORIZON
==========================================

These are evidence triggers, not scheduled promises. Revisit only when browser
APIs, model capabilities, authoritative components, or well-designed
experiments create new evidence.

Multimodal on-device page understanding
  Owner: ML/privacy lead with accessibility and browser engineers.
  Goal: distinguish ambiguous ad containers using local visual, DOM, and
  accessibility signals.
  Evaluation: unseen pages, devices, languages, adversarial layouts, wanted-
  content precision, latency, memory, battery, and accessibility task success.
  Success: meaningful incremental coverage over deterministic rules within
  strict local-device and false-positive budgets.
  Stop: page upload, excessive resource use, unexplainable decisions, or
  legitimate content loss is required.

Reversible layout repair after safe removal
  Owner: browser UX/accessibility lead.
  Goal: close empty gaps without moving, hiding, or disabling wanted controls.
  Evaluation: layout stability, task completion, cumulative layout shift,
  screen-reader order, focus, interaction, and reliable undo.
  Success: visible gaps improve while page tasks and accessibility remain at
  least as reliable as the no-repair baseline.
  Stop: accessibility, focus, interaction, or layout regressions cannot be
  bounded and reversed.

Human-approved rule-operations agent
  Owner: release/rules operator.
  Goal: prepare an explained candidate update, run deterministic gates, and
  summarize risks. It never signs or promotes a release by itself.
  Evaluation: gate completeness, explanation accuracy, missed-risk rate,
  reviewer time, rejected suggestions, and staged rollback outcomes.
  Success: reviewers make faster, better-supported decisions without reduced
  scrutiny or autonomous promotion.
  Stop: explanations are unreliable, severe risks are missed, or reviewers
  become rubber stamps.

Privacy-preserving compatibility learning
  Owner: security/privacy and ML leads, with independent audit.
  Goal: learn from explicitly opted-in, minimized signals without raw URLs,
  page text, screenshots, or histories.
  Evaluation: measurable compatibility benefit, re-identification testing,
  consent comprehension, collection minimization, retention, and deletion.
  Success: audited minimized signals improve reproducible compatibility cases
  without creating a browsing-history dataset.
  Stop: de-identification is weak, consent is unclear, deletion is unreliable,
  or central data creates disproportionate risk.

Accessible mobile-browser protection
  Owner: dedicated mobile platform lead.
  Goal: determine what platform APIs genuinely permit, with separate privacy,
  battery, store-policy, and accessibility tests. It is not an automatic port.
  Evaluation: supported request/cosmetic behavior, wanted-content preservation,
  device and battery overhead, accessibility tasks, privacy, and store policy.
  Success: one defined mobile target delivers useful protection within native
  constraints and has a maintainable signing/release path.
  Stop: the platform cannot provide meaningful safe coverage, resource or
  accessibility budgets fail, store policy forbids the design, or no owner can
  maintain it.

Separate desktop and DNS products
  Owner: separately funded platform/security leads.
  Goal: evaluate system-wide or network-wide protection with independent threat
  models, deployment, updates, observability, support, and legal review. These
  products should not be hidden inside the browser-extension scope.
  Evaluation: coverage and false positives by protocol/application, privilege
  level, attack surface, latency, operational complexity, support, and cost.
  Success: a separately governed prototype provides clear user value with a
  defensible security, privacy, update, and funding model.
  Stop: required privileges or data visibility are disproportionate, encrypted
  traffic cannot be handled safely, costs are unsustainable, or ownership is
  missing.

Server-inserted video-ad research
  Owner: video research lead.
  Goal: revisit only if a reproducible, policy-compliant signal distinguishes
  ads from wanted content without DRM/paywall circumvention or harmful skips.
  Evaluation: exact stream category, playback integrity, wanted-media false
  skips, account/region effects, latency, privacy, and policy compliance.
  Success: a narrow repeatable category can be handled without harming wanted
  media, accounts, privacy, accessibility, or policy compliance.
  Stop: streams are technically indistinguishable, wanted playback is harmed,
  or the method requires circumvention. “Future AI will solve it” is not
  evidence.

Community and regional rule stewardship
  Owner: community/rules lead plus regional maintainers.
  Goal: use AI to assist review queues, never replace local expertise.
  Evaluation: reviewer agreement, reproduced coverage gains, false positives,
  time-to-repair, participation health, and per-region maintenance cost.
  Success: qualified local reviewers sustainably improve a versioned regional
  corpus with lower breakage and clear provenance.
  Stop: local review is unavailable, abuse dominates, data rights are unclear,
  or the region cannot be supported without misleading users.

Browser-vendor collaboration
  Owner: technical/product lead.
  Goal: advocate for safer, privacy-preserving filtering, diagnostics, and
  update APIs.
  Evaluation: concrete proposals, vendor feedback, prototype API value,
  permission/privacy impact, standards progress, and implementability.
  Success: a documented vendor or standards change enables safer measurable
  capability without expanding surveillance or weakening browser security.
  Stop: proposals require invasive access, conflict with user safety, receive
  no actionable path after scheduled reviews, or consume effort needed for the
  supported core. Success depends on vendor decisions, not only project code.


18. VIDEO AND YOUTUBE-SPECIFIC POLICY
=====================================

The project will treat “video ads” as a matrix of technical categories rather
than a single promise:

  - ordinary third-party network requests;
  - page overlays and companion banners;
  - client-side inserted media segments;
  - creator sponsorship segments;
  - server-side inserted streams;
  - authenticated, encrypted, account- or region-dependent delivery;
  - anti-blocking and playback integrity behavior.

Each category needs its own owner, lawful and policy-compliant method, test
accounts where permitted, regions, browsers, negative controls, playback
integrity checks, and stop conditions. Sponsor skipping, if ever considered,
requires separate API/database licensing, user consent, and product policy.

The release language must always say “best effort on tested categories” unless
future evidence supports a narrower stronger statement. It must never promise
all YouTube ads are blocked forever.


19. PLATFORM EXPANSION POLICY
=============================

Chrome first
  Stabilize one complete build, test, update, release, support, and rollback
  process before broad expansion.

Edge
  Likely shares Chromium concepts but still needs packaging, store, permission,
  update, enterprise-policy, and regression verification.

Firefox
  May offer different network APIs and capabilities. Use an explicit adapter
  and independent test matrix; do not pretend Chrome MV3 behavior is identical.

Safari and mobile browsers
  Require separate feasibility work, signing, platform APIs, performance,
  battery, privacy, and accessibility review.

Desktop and DNS
  Are distinct products, not extension features. Each needs separate staffing,
  threat modeling, updates, support, and user-consent design.


20. ACCOUNTABILITY MODEL — WHO WILL DO THE WORK
================================================

Product owner / founder
  Owns audience, scope, brand, budget, distribution model, project license,
  public claims, and final go/no-go decisions.

Technical/architecture lead
  Owns one coherent runtime, module boundaries, architecture decisions, and
  the rule against uncontrolled engine overlap.

Browser-extension engineer
  Owns manifest, service worker, content scripts, controls, settings, browser
  adapters, and API correctness.

Filtering and rule-data steward
  Owns source provenance, parser/compiler behavior, syntax, priority, quotas,
  data updates, regional packs, and rollback inputs.

Compatibility and QA lead
  Owns synthetic fixtures, representative-site corpus, breakage severity,
  browser matrix, regressions, benchmark reproducibility, and triage.

Security and privacy lead
  Owns permissions, data flow, threat model, supply chain, content-script
  boundaries, vulnerability response, and privacy review.

License/provenance reviewer or qualified counsel
  Owns code, data, asset, model, dependency, notice, contribution, and
  distribution-right review. An AI agent cannot fill this legal role.

UX and accessibility lead
  Owns understandable controls, onboarding, recovery, keyboard behavior,
  screen-reader behavior, color/contrast, and honest diagnostics.

Release and operations owner
  Owns CI, reproducible artifacts, checksums, signing, staged rollout, update
  monitoring, incident response, rollback drills, and store operations.

AI/ML research lead
  Owns bounded experiments, data minimization, baseline comparisons, model
  versioning, resource budgets, drift review, and stop decisions. This role may
  not promote experiments unilaterally.

Regional/native-language maintainers
  Own local corpus validity, regional rules, translations, wanted-content
  review, and sustainable follow-up for their regions.

Support/triage owner
  Owns user-facing issue intake, redaction, reproduction, severity, response,
  and escalation without creating a browsing-history database.

A small early team may combine several roles, but every active task and every
research record must name one accountable person. “The community” or “AI” is
not an accountable owner.


21. EVALUATION SCORECARD
========================

Blocking efficacy
  Eligible controlled ad/tracker requests blocked; missed cases categorized by
  syntax, quota, timing, permissions, and delivery type.

Wanted-content precision
  Legitimate controls, requests, media, text, authentication, checkout, and
  accessibility behavior preserved.

Website breakage
  Severity-weighted failures per tested site/task, not just a raw site count.

Recovery
  Time and steps to pause, trust a site, undo a rule, restore settings, roll
  back a bundle, and return to safe operation.

Performance
  Incremental CPU, memory, page-load time, long-task time, rule footprint,
  package size, and, where relevant, battery use versus no-extension baseline.

Privacy
  Manifest permissions, host access, stored data, retention, expected outbound
  traffic, unexpected outbound traffic, report minimization, and consent.

Security and supply chain
  Dependency inventory, advisories, reproducibility, artifact hashes, remote
  code checks, update integrity, secret handling, and unresolved findings.

Rule/update reliability
  Compilation determinism, unsupported syntax, quota utilization, update
  success, corrupted-update rejection, offline behavior, and rollback success.

Accessibility and usability
  Keyboard and screen-reader task completion, focus, contrast, error recovery,
  permission comprehension, and success on common basic/advanced tasks.

Regional coverage
  Results by region and language with qualified review, not a global average
  that hides unsupported areas.

Support and maintenance
  Time-to-reproduce, time-to-repair, open severe regressions, update workload,
  reviewer effort, regional ownership, and recurring operational cost.

AI experiment value
  Improvement over a deterministic/manual baseline, false positives, reviewer
  acceptance, time saved, privacy impact, resource cost, drift, and rollback.

Every result should identify date, source commit/version, browser/version,
operating system/hardware class, rules and settings, corpus version, repetitions,
failures, uncertainty, and omitted cases. Numerical targets should be set only
after honest baselines exist.


22. FEATURE AND EVIDENCE LIFECYCLE
==================================

Idea
  A user problem and possible approach are written down. No shipping claim.

Research
  An owner tests a falsifiable hypothesis and records evidence, risks, and stop
  conditions.

Prototype
  Isolated code demonstrates a narrow behavior. It is disabled or separate
  from normal users and has an explicit removal path.

Controlled verification
  Deterministic fixtures, negative controls, representative cases, performance,
  privacy, accessibility, security, and license gates are run.

Release candidate
  Reproducible artifact, migration, rollback, support, known limits, and owner
  approval are complete.

Supported
  Staged release is monitored, documentation matches behavior, a maintainer is
  accountable, and incidents can be rolled back.

Deprecated or rejected
  The reason, migration/removal, preserved findings, failed approaches, and
  next evidence trigger are recorded. Rejection is a valid research result.


23. RELEASE GATES AND ROLLBACK
==============================

No release should advance merely because a build file exists. Required gates:

  - reproducible source and dependency installation;
  - active tests appropriate to changed behavior;
  - exact target-browser verification;
  - benign negative controls and breakage review;
  - performance and rule-budget review;
  - permissions, privacy, and unexpected-network review;
  - security, dependency, and update-path review;
  - source/data/model license and notice review;
  - accessibility and recovery checks;
  - settings migration and rollback verification;
  - artifact checksum and staged rollout plan;
  - known-limitations and support documentation;
  - accountable release owner approval.

Rollback must cover application releases, settings migrations where feasible,
filter bundles, model experiments, and remote data. A rollout without a tested
last-known-good path is not release ready.

25. COSTS AND PRACTICAL RESOURCES
=================================

The largest recurring cost is compatibility maintenance. Websites, advertising
delivery, browsers, policies, rules, and attacks change continuously.

Plan for:

  - extension and rules engineering;
  - code, security, privacy, and license review;
  - browser/device and accessibility testing;
  - CI, artifact storage, release signing, and update delivery;
  - representative and regional test maintenance;
  - user support, issue triage, and incident response;
  - vulnerability and dependency maintenance;
  - store accounts, policy work, and release operations;
  - optional model training/evaluation and supported test hardware;
  - qualified legal review when distribution obligations are uncertain.

No paid service is enabled by this idea. Spending, dates, staffing, and public
launch must be decided by the owner. AI experiments must demonstrate benefit
over simpler approaches after compute, device, privacy, and reviewer costs are
included.

Possible future sustainability models include donations, sponsorship that does
not control filtering decisions, paid support, managed organizational policy,
or optional premium convenience features. No model is approved. Selling
browsing data, paid allowlisting hidden from users, or weakening protection for
advertisers would violate the trust position.


26. MAJOR RISKS AND RESPONSES
=============================

License or provenance failure
  Response: quarantine by default; immutable source records; qualified review;
  no active integration until approved.

Site breakage from aggressive filtering
  Response: negative controls, representative task testing, severity scoring,
  staged rules, site trust, undo, and last-known-good rollback.

Browser policy/API change
  Response: official-source monitoring, target-version CI, adapter boundaries,
  honest capability changes, and no unsupported bypasses.

Compromised update or dependency
  Response: pinned dependencies, SBOM, integrity checks, reproducible builds,
  least privilege, staged release, protected signing, and rollback.

Privacy erosion through support or AI
  Response: local defaults, minimization, previewable opt-in reports, retention
  limits, access controls, privacy review, and no raw history collection.

AI false confidence
  Response: deterministic baselines, held-out evaluation, human approval,
  limited experiments, clear uncertainty, and preset stop conditions.

Maintenance overload
  Response: one pipeline, narrow supported scope, named owners, severity-based
  triage, regional expansion only with maintainers, and rejection of features
  whose recurring cost is not sustainable.

Misleading marketing
  Response: evidence-linked claims, version/date labels, limitations, and
  explicit separation of source present, tested, experimental, and planned.

29. THE LONG-TERM PRODUCT COMMITMENT
====================================

We will build protection that is useful, explainable, reversible, and worthy
of user trust. We will treat missed ads and broken wanted content as measurable
engineering problems. We will keep ordinary operation local, make data and
permissions visible, preserve safe recovery, and publish only what evidence
supports.

We will treat video advertising, regional coverage, cross-browser behavior,
and future AI as collections of specific research questions, not magical
promises. We will preserve failed experiments because they prevent repeated
mistakes. We will stop work that cannot meet privacy, safety, legal, performance,
or maintenance gates even when the idea sounds impressive.

The ambition may be worldwide. Every supported release must earn its claims
through reproducible source, exact-version browser behavior, representative
evidence, wanted-content protection, accessibility, secure updates, privacy
safeguards, license clarity, accountable maintenance, and tested rollback.

That combination—effective protection, low breakage, privacy, honesty, and a
disciplined AI-assisted maintenance system—is the project’s best opportunity
to become genuinely valuable in the AI era.
